Assistant: This touches on a core architectural debate: **Filesystem vs. Database.**

The current "Markdown-first" approach is a deliberate choice influenced by Karpathy’s "LLM OS" and the "Mnemonic Light" philosophy, but your instinct about the DB is correct for several operational reasons.

Here is a breakdown of why we use `.md` files now and what we would gain (and lose) by going direct-to-DB.

### Why we use `.md` Files (The "Light" Way)
1.  **Human Legibility:** You can open your `raw/` folder in Obsidian, VS Code, or even the macOS Finder and read your sources without needing a special tool or running the `api.ts`.
2.  **Git-Powered:** You get version history and backups for free. If you accidentally delete a "brain," you can just `git checkout` your raw sources.
3.  **The "Nuclear" Recovery:** If `brain.db` gets corrupted or deleted, the brain can be 100% reconstructed from the files (which is what `internalRebuildIndex` does).
4.  **Simplicity for Agents:** It is very easy for an LLM to "read" a file. It’s a bit more complex for it to construct and execute a SQL `INSERT` statement correctly every time.

### Why Direct-to-DB is tempting (The "Hardcore" Way)
1.  **Zero "Sync" Friction:** You wouldn't need the `internalRebuildIndex` step. The moment a piece of data is added, it is searchable.
2.  **Speed/Concurrency:** Databases are built to handle thousands of small writes. Managing 10,000 small `.md` files can eventually slow down the OS filesystem.
3.  **Relational Power:** You can enforce that every piece of raw data *must* have a valid `project` and `source_type` at the schema level. Folders are "loose."
4.  **Transaction Safety:** You can ensure that an "ingest" and "index" happen in one atomic transaction—either both succeed or both fail.

### The "Middle Path": The Best of Both Worlds
Instead of choosing one or the other, we could move to a **"DB-Primary, File-Secondary"** model.

*   **How it would work:** The `add` command and `ingest` tools write directly into the SQLite `raw_entries` table. 
*   **The "Shadow" Filesystem:** Mnemonic could then automatically "export" or "sync" those DB rows back to the `raw/` folder as `.md` files in the background.
*   **The Result:** You get the speed and searchability of the DB for the agent, but you still have a folder full of Markdown files for yourself.

**My Opinion:**
If we move to **Mnemonic Hardcore** (the Rust track), using a DB (like PGLite or Tabularium's native store) as the primary storage is the right move for performance. For **Mnemonic Light**, keeping the `.md` files as the primary source keeps the project "hackable" and transparent.

### 2. The Structured Event Schema

*   `id`: Internal PK.
*   `type`: (e.g., `user_msg`, `ai_msg`, `tool_call`, `tool_resp`, `exit_interview`).
